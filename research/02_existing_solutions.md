Research date: 27-09-2026. Track: prior art, feasibility, false-novelty check, and failure lessons.

## 0. Bottom line (read this first)

1. **Data-verified certificate issuance with no fresh documents already exists in India.**
   - **Haryana** has run it since **Sep 2022**. Income certificates are issued over the counter from verified family data (PPP/FIDR). Caste certificates are issued when caste is already verified in FIDR.
   - **Karnataka** has "e-Kshana" instant certificates and the **Kutumba** family database, which fetches caste and income for eligibility checks.
   - **Andhra Pradesh:**
     - G.O.Ms.469 (2023) bans departments from demanding a fresh caste certificate.
     - In 2025, 28.62 lakh families got permanent caste certificates after the state reviewed data for 34.37 lakh families.

   **We must NOT claim that "nobody in India uses state data to verify certificates".** The existing plan's line "Nobody in India does this at the application stage" is too strong. Haryana effectively runs a rule-based two-lane system: data-verified certificates are issued over the counter, and the rest go to local-committee verification.
2. **Risk-lane triage (GREEN/AMBER) is a mature Indian government pattern.** Examples:
   - Customs RMS: 90% facilitation since Jul 2021.
   - GST risk-based biometric and physical verification.
   - Income-tax CASS.
   - Passport Post-Police-Verification (2016).
   - EPFO auto-settlement: 71% of advance claims in FY26.

   These are strong feasibility precedents. I found **no deployed example of risk-lane triage for state caste, income or domicile certificate approval.**
3. **Family-lineage caste evidence has legal backing.**
   - In Maharashtra, a blood relative's validity certificate is treated as near-conclusive: SC 2023, and the Rules were amended in 2017.
   - Delhi accepts a paternal blood relative's certificate.

   I found **no automated system** that links an applicant to the family's past certificates and flags conflicts. This is the most defensible "new" piece.
4. **AI-drafted orders are legally dangerous right now.**
   - **Gujarat HC (Aug 2026)** struck down a chain of four GST orders built on AI-hallucinated citations.
   - **The Supreme Court (3 Sep 2026)** set aside a Rs 425 cr customs penalty for the same reason.

   The "drafted reasoned order" must cite **only internal records, never case law**. It must be template-bound, and the officer must sign off on every line.
5. **The global failures** are Robodebt, MiDAS, the Dutch toeslagenaffaire and SyRI, Telangana Samagra Vedika, Brazil INSS and the DWP bias findings. All point to the same design rules:
   - no automated rejection
   - the state carries the burden of proof
   - no protected attributes or proxies as risk features
   - fairness monitoring
   - an explainable evidence trail
   - a legal basis for linking data

### 4b. What appears GENUINELY NEW (to our knowledge, after about 70 searches)
1. **An officer-side decision-support copilot at the approval stage of state caste, income and domicile certificates.** Existing Indian "AI" in e-District (Delhi 2.0, AP Mana Mitra) is citizen-facing chat or delivery. Existing data-driven issuance (Haryana, Karnataka) is rule-based pre-verification without per-case evidence cards.
2. **A family-lineage caste consistency check:**
   - it links an applicant to relatives' previously issued certificates in the state archive
   - it flags conflicts, for example a different caste or category from the father's certificate, or a sibling rejected
   - it presents the match to the officer as the legal "blood-relative" evidence the courts recognise
3. **A per-application evidence card** that combines land records, the ration roster, the farmer registry and the prior-certificate archive, with each claim traced to its source record.
4. **Reason-coded draft orders** (approve, reject, or request information) that cite only internal records. This is designed against the 2026 Gujarat HC and SC hallucination rulings.
5. **Consistency analytics across districts.** A 5.7× variance in rejection rates under the same rules becomes a target for standardising scrutiny. I found no Indian certificate system publishing or acting on this.

## 5. Design lessons (each tied to evidence)

1. **No automated adverse decision, ever.** AMBER means "officer looks closer" or "ask the applicant for a document". It never means reject. *(Robodebt; MiDAS; Brazil STJ 9 Sep 2026; SC on Telangana: "You cannot cancel before verification")*
2. **Missing data ≠ ineligible.** Rural and ST applicants are under-represented in databases. Absence of a record should route to *request information*, not to a negative flag. *(Brazil INSS rural denials; AP left 5 lakh families uncovered after the data review)*
3. **Show evidence, not a score.** Show each matched record side by side with a match-confidence score and its source, so the officer can see a wrong match. *(Samagra Vedika: rickshaw puller matched to a car owner; Haryana PPP income errors)*
4. **No protected attributes or proxies as risk features.** Do not use caste, tribe, religion, gender or district/village as fraud features. Caste is the *subject* of the certificate, not a risk factor. *(Dutch nationality factor; Denmark "Model Abroad"; France CAF)*
5. **Fairness and automation-bias monitoring.** Track AMBER rate, rejection rate and officer override rate by district and category. Publish a short fairness note. *(DWP fairness assessment found disparities and did not measure automation bias)*
6. **Randomly audit the GREEN lane.** Sample, for example, 2-5% of GREEN cases for full manual review. This measures false negatives and deters gaming. *(Customs RMS "element of randomness … retained"; CG's own random verification of school caste certificates [U]; passport PPV verifies after issue)*
7. **LLM drafting guardrails:**
   - no case law or statutory citations generated by the model
   - templates approved by the Revenue Department
   - every sentence cites an internal record ID
   - a validator blocks any unknown reference
   - the officer must edit and sign

   *(Gujarat HC Aug 2026; SC, Vijay Ghanshyam Gadiya, 3 Sep 2026)*
8. **Legal basis for data linking.** Get a GO or executive order authorising cross-department lookups for certificate verification, together with purpose limitation under the DPDP Act 2023. *(SyRI struck down; Haryana has the PPP Act 2021; Karnataka is giving Kutumba legal cover)*
9. **No black-box vendors.** Rules and model logic should be open to audit and RTI. *(Posidex / Samagra Vedika RTI refusal)*
10. **Tell citizens what data was used**, and give a one-click correction route when a registry is wrong. *(DWP Whitemail criticism; Haryana PPP correction camps)*
11. **Prefer deterministic rules first, ML second.** EPFO's high straight-through rate comes from clear rule checks (KYC, seeding), which are explainable and defensible.
12. **Pre-register metrics and pilot in 1-2 districts.** Metrics:
    - rejection rate
    - appeals and re-applications
    - officer minutes per case
    - override rate
    - GREEN-audit error rate

    Choose districts at both ends of the rejection range, such as GPM at 13.2% and Bijapur at 2.3%. *(Nava's "inconclusive due to small sample"; Singapore reports hours saved)*
13. **Build inside the state stack** (NIC/CHiPS, as a sidecar to Sewa Setu or e-District), not as an external dependency. *(Benefits Data Trust collapse)*
14. **Pair with validity reforms.** Lifetime caste validity (AP, Odisha, Haryana, MP) and multi-year income validity (Gujarat 3 years) reduce volume. Present these as a policy recommendation next to the tool. This also links to the PS2 runner-up.
15. **Assisted channel first.** Most CG applications come through operators or CSCs. The copilot must also help the *operator* fix missing evidence before submission. *(Brazil: digital-literacy barriers)*
