# 03 · Chhattisgarh policy, institutional, legal and administrative environment for "Nirnay Sahayak"
Research date: 27-09-2026. Scope: does the CG policy, institutional and legal environment support or block an AI decision-support copilot for designated officers approving income, domicile and caste certificates on Sewa Setu?

**Legend.** **[V]** means verified from the fetched source. **[U]** means unverified, second-hand, or from memory. Keep [U] items off slides, or soften them.

---

## TL;DR
- **Policy tailwind is strong and very recent.** The CM's AI review on 1–2 Jul 2026 named five AI Mission pillars, one of which is "AI in governance". It explicitly said the state would deploy **"AI-based decision support systems"** and appoint **AI nodal officers in every department**, each with its own roadmap. The Cabinet then approved the **₹500 cr / 5-year CG State AI Mission** on 5 Aug 2026. That mission targets **50+ AI-based government services** and AI training for **1.5 lakh government employees**. The Electronics & IT Department and CHiPS run it.
- **The legal design must stay "assistive, not deciding".** Certificate decisions are statutory and quasi-judicial:
  - The Caste Act 2013 gives the Competent Authority the powers of a civil court.
  - The officer must apply their own mind, so a decision in substance taken by someone else (or by a machine) is void.
  - Courts insist on reasons (Kranti Associates; Madhuri Patil).
  - Courts are currently hostile to AI making decisions or drafting findings unchecked (Kerala HC policy of Jul 2025; SC remarks of Dec 2025).
  - A copilot that surfaces evidence and pre-drafts a speaking order, with the officer signing, is consistent with this. Auto-approve or auto-reject is not.
- **The law already contemplates the data we would cross-check.** CG Caste Rules 2013, Rule 3(3), accept as evidence revenue records, jamabandi, and a *"Caste Certificate issued earlier to Father, Ancestor or a relative"*. Rule 8 requires enquiry into residence, revenue records and immovable property. Rule 15(2) makes District Verification Committees verify about **10% of certificates by random sampling**. That is a ready hook for *risk-based* sampling instead of random sampling.
- **The officer burden is real and documented:**
  - Tehsildar strikes in Jul–Aug 2025 (**20,000+ files stuck**; demands included staff and technical shortages) and Jun 2026 (Sushasan Tihar and certificate work halted).
  - **911 Patwari and 393 RI vacancies** being filled (Jan 2026).
  - Dhamtari tehsil: **400+ certificate applications a day against about 200 disposed**.
- **Fraud evidence fits the design exactly.** 267 of 659 investigated caste certificates were found fake (GAD, Aug 2026). In the 2026 fake-Baiga cases (Bilaspur, GPM), *revenue records, school records and electoral papers showed OBC/other castes*. That is precisely the cross-record inconsistency Nirnay Sahayak would flag.
- **Main legal risks:**
  - DPDP Rules 2025, Rule 5 and Second Schedule. They are binding from **13 May 2027** and require purpose limitation, minimisation, accuracy, notice and accountability for State processing for certificates.
  - The Caste Act, s.12, criminal liability for officers, which cuts both ways.
  - The HC holding (22 Jul 2026) that only SDO (Revenue) and above can issue caste certificates, not Tehsildars.
  - No published CG state data-sharing policy yet. The national framework (CoS, 15 May 2026) requires State Data Governance Committees chaired by the Chief Secretary.

## 4. Legal basis and constraints for the design

### 4.1 CG Lok Sewa Guarantee Adhiniyam 2011 (Act 23 of 2011)
- **s.3:** a right to notified services "within the stipulated time".
- **s.4:** the designated officer must deliver in time; applications must be acknowledged. Cost for delay is reportedly **₹100 a day, capped at ₹1,000** [V: IndianKanoon summary; U: verify against the latest amendment]. It is recoverable after notice and hearing and paid to the applicant as compensation.
- **s.5:** a Competent Officer imposes the cost.
- **s.7:** appeal within 30 days, to be decided in 45 days. Orders go into the officer's service record and can lead to disciplinary action.
- **Written reasons for rejection are not expressly required in the Act text we could read.** [V: legitquest reading; U: the Rules/Niyam 2011 may require it] The obligation to give reasons comes from **natural justice and case law** (see 4.8). Seva Setu now automates the penalty calculation (ThePrint).
- Sources: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/149498322/ ; https://www.legitquest.com/act/chhattisgarh-lok-sewa-guarantee-act-2011/4454
- **Implication:** officers are squeezed on both sides. They face *delay penalties* (LSG), and *criminal liability for wrongly issued caste certificates* (Caste Act s.12). A copilot that speeds up genuine cases **and** documents due diligence helps with both.

### 4.2 CG SC, ST & OBC (Regulation of Social Status Certification) Act 2013 and Rules 2013

**The Act:**
- **s.2 Competent Authority.** An officer designated by the government.
  - **Chhattisgarh HC, 22 Jul 2026** (Justice Parth Pratim Sahu, Shailey Sonkar case): **Tehsildars are not competent** to issue caste certificates. The competent authority is generally the **SDO (Revenue)**, or Collector, Addl. Collector or Sub-Collector.
  - Tehsildars and Naib Tehsildars issue only *temporary* certificates.
  - Source: https://www.haribhoomi.com/state-local/chhattishgarh/news/bilaspur-cg-high-court-caste-certificate-sdo-authority-tehsildar-order-105307
- **s.3–4:** application, and issuance after inquiry. The certificate is **permanent**.
- **s.5:** appeal within 30 days, decided within 3 months.
- **s.6:** District Level Certificate Verification Committee (suo motu or on reference; 1 month).
- **s.7–8:** High Power Certification Scrutiny Committee can cancel certificates. Its order is final subject only to Art. 226.
- **s.9:** consequences of cancellation: admission void, job lost, benefits recovered as land revenue, degrees cancelled.
- **s.10:** false certificate offences: RI 3 months–2 years and a ₹2,000–20,000 fine; cognizable and non-bailable.
- **s.12: officer liability.** A competent authority that **intentionally** issues a false certificate faces the same punishment. **Due diligence based on affidavits is protected.**
- **s.15:** the **burden of proof is on the applicant**.
- Enquiring authorities have **civil-court powers**, which makes this a **quasi-judicial** function.
- Sources: https://www.casemine.com/act/in/5ed4fb22894ef23297d8c15f ; https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/12777?locale=en

**The Rules 2013** (https://indiankanoon.org/doc/156162861/):
- **Rule 3(2):** application may be made through a **CHOICE / Common Service Centre** (the digital channel is legally recognised).
- **Rule 3(3):** requires an affidavit (Form 2A) and a **family tree covering 3 generations issued by the Halka Patwari**. Also required is proof of ancestors' residence before the notification dates: **SC 10 Aug 1950, ST 6 Sep 1950, OBC 26 Dec 1984**. Acceptable proof includes:
  - revenue records, jamabandi, the 1931 Census, and birth and death registers
  - **"Caste Certificate, issued earlier to Father, Ancestor or a relative"**
  - **This is the legal hook for the family-lineage check.**
  - OBC applicants also need the father's income certificate (creamy layer).
- **Rule 7:** the Competent Officer may inquire himself or **direct a subordinate Revenue Officer**. Delegating the *inquiry* is lawful; the *decision* is not delegated.
- **Rule 8:** the inquiry covers **residence, permanent address, revenue records, immovable property and occupation**. Oral evidence from village officials and existing certificate holders of the caste can be taken.
- **Rules 9–10:** issue within **1 month**. A provisional certificate is issued in 15 days.
- **Rule 14:** District Verification Committee, chaired by an Addl. or Deputy Collector.
- **Rule 15(2):** the committee verifies **about 10% of certificates by random sampling**. This is an **opening for AI risk-scored sampling**. Pitch it as "make the 10% count".
- **Rule 18:** reasons for non-satisfaction must be communicated.
- **Rule 20:** a **vigilance cell** (DySP) investigates suspected false certificates.

**Verdict:** the law **explicitly permits evidence from existing state records and relatives' certificates**. A tool that retrieves and presents them is squarely within the enquiry the officer must already conduct. The tool must **not** replace the Rule 8 enquiry (field or oral evidence) where the records are insufficient.

### 4.3 Income and domicile certificates: legal basis
- These are issued under **executive instructions** (GAD / Revenue circulars and the Revenue Book Circular), not a dedicated statute. They are notified under the LSG Act. [U: we could not fetch the exact RBC or GAD circular numbers]
- The Sewa Setu page names the "Revenue and Disaster Management Department" as authority. In practice the Tehsildar or Naib Tehsildar signs. [U]
- **CG Land Revenue Code 1959:** creates the offices of Tehsildar, Naib Tehsildar, Patwari and RI, and provides for land records (the Bhuiyan legal basis). [U: section-level detail not fetched]

### 4.4 IT Act 2000 and e-Sign
- **s.4–5** give legal recognition to electronic records and signatures. **s.3A and the Second Schedule** allow Aadhaar e-Sign. [U: from memory; standard law]
- CHiPS is currently procuring an **e-Sign Service Provider for e-District** [V]. Our "officer e-signs the final order" step fits the existing trajectory.

### 4.5 DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025
- **Rules notified 13 Nov 2025.** Rules 1, 2 and 17–21 are immediate (Data Protection Board). **Rule 4** (consent managers) applies from **13 Nov 2026**. **Rules 3, 5–16, 22 and 23 apply from 13 May 2027.** [V]
  - https://cadp.in/resources/official-texts/dpdp-rules-2025/
  - https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf
- **Rule 5:** processing by the State "for subsidy, benefit, service, **certificate**, licence or permit" (under law, policy or public funds) under the **s.7(b) legitimate use** must follow the **Second Schedule** standards. [V] https://dpdprules.org/rules/5 The standards are:
  1. lawful processing
  2. specified purposes only
  3. only necessary data
  4. reasonable efforts at accuracy and consistency
  5. retention only as needed
  6. reasonable security safeguards
  7. notice to the data principal with a contact point and a route to rights
  8. accountability
- **Rule 6:** security safeguards, including **logs retained for 1 year**. [V]
- **Implication:**
  - No fresh consent is needed. The state can process for certificates under s.7(b).
  - But the cross-check must be **purpose-limited**: query only fields relevant to the certificate type, with no fishing across all databases.
  - Every data pull must be logged and attributable.
  - The applicant must be told which records were checked, and any adverse data point must be disclosed in the order so they can contest it (the accuracy standard).
  - Build these features now. They become **mandatory in about 7.5 months** (May 2027), which is a strong "future-proof" slide line.

### 4.7 National data, AI governance and security frameworks

**National Framework for Data Sharing** (CoS chaired by the Cabinet Secretary, **15 May 2026**) [V], https://negd.gov.in/ndg/. Four pillars:
1. Data-sharing policies by every Ministry **and State**, with consent, de-identification and classification.
2. Standardised data, metadata and APIs, with wrappers for legacy systems.
3. Adoption of API Setu, NAPIX, **AI Kosh**, IUDX, DigiLocker and Entity Locker.
4. NDGC at the Centre and **State Data Governance Committees chaired by Chief Secretaries**.

→ The CG SDGC is the body that would approve the cross-department pulls from Bhuiyan, Khadya, AgriStack and Sewa Setu. **No CG state data policy was found yet** [U]. Pitch: "our API-wrapper design is compliant with the national framework; a pilot needs an SDGC data-sharing approval."

**NDSAP 2012:** pre-existing and still relevant for classifying shareable versus restricted data. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Data_Sharing_and_Accessibility_Policy

**India AI Governance Guidelines** (MeitY, 5 Nov 2025) [V]. They are voluntary and advisory. https://chambers.com/articles/meity-unveils-india-s-approach-towards-regulating-artificial-intelligence

The seven sutras:
1. Trust is the foundation.
2. **People first.** "Humans should, as far as possible, have final control over AI systems."
3. Innovation over restraint.
4. **Fairness and equity.**
5. **Accountability.**
6. **Understandable by design.**
7. Safety, resilience and sustainability.

### 4.8 Natural justice, reasoned orders, and limits on AI in adjudication
- **Kranti Associates v. Masood Ahmed Khan, (2010) 9 SCC 496:** every quasi-judicial order must be supported by reasons, since "reason is the soul of justice". Reasons also restrain arbitrary administrative power. [V] https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1304475/
- **Kumari Madhuri Patil v. Addl. Commissioner (1994):** the landmark caste-certificate verification framework. Scrutiny committees must give an opportunity to be heard and pass orders "with brief reasons". [V] https://indiankanoon.org/doc/799713/
- **Anirudhsinhji Jadeja v. State of Gujarat (1995):** when statutory power is conferred on one authority but in substance exercised by another, the decision is **ultra vires and void**. [V] https://indiankanoon.org/doc/256001/ → **The officer must genuinely decide.** The AI must not "dictate". We need UI friction against rubber-stamping, for example:
  - the officer must tick or override each flag
  - edits are logged
  - no one-click approval of AI-flagged cases
- **Kerala HC "Policy on Use of AI Tools in District Judiciary" (Jul 2025):** AI must not be used "to arrive at findings, reliefs, orders or judgments". AI may be used only as an assistive tool, and only approved tools may be used. [V] https://images.assettype.com/theleaflet/2025-07-22/mt4bw6n7/Kerala_HC_AI_Guidelines.pdf
- **Supreme Court (Dec 2025):** the CJI said AI will never be allowed to overpower judicial decision-making, and reconstituted the SC AI Committee. [V] https://www.newsonair.gov.in/supreme-court-assures-judges-exercisingutmost-caution-on-use-of-ai-in-judicial-process/
- **Design implications of the Kerala HC policy:**
  - Revenue officers deciding caste claims are quasi-judicial, so the same caution applies by analogy.
  - Present the draft order as an **editable template populated with verified facts and cited records**, not as AI "findings".
  - Every factual statement in the draft must link to a source record (no free-text hallucination).
  - The order is signed by the officer and must state that the officer examined the records.

## 7. Implications for the proposal

### 7.1 What supports it
1. **Explicit policy hook.** The CG AI Mission (Jul/Aug 2026) commits to "AI-based decision support systems" in departments, AI nodal officers, 50+ AI services and ₹500 cr. Nirnay Sahayak is an off-the-shelf candidate for the Revenue dept's first DSS.
2. **The statute already names our data sources.** Caste Rules 2013, Rule 3(3), accept relatives' certificates, revenue records and jamabandi. Rule 8 mandates checking revenue records and property. Rule 15(2) mandates 10% verification. **We automate evidence-gathering the law already requires.**
3. **Institutional fit:**
   - CHiPS owns Sewa Setu and the SDC (on-prem private cloud), so self-hosted models are feasible.
   - The e-Sign SP procurement is live.
   - IIIT-NR CM IT Fellows (process mining, AI) are available as a build and maintenance workforce.
   - The Good Governance dept and AMP can consume the district-variance analytics.
4. **Pain is proven and recent:**
   - strikes in 2025 and 2026
   - 20k+ stuck files
   - 911 Patwari vacancies
   - Dhamtari's 2:1 inflow-to-disposal ratio
   - 267 fake certificates
   - the 2026 Baiga fraud caught *after* jobs and admissions were lost
5. **National frameworks align:**
   - AI Governance Guidelines ("people first", human final control)
   - DPDP Rule 5 (State may process for certificates)
   - National Data Sharing Framework (states must build data-sharing procedures and APIs)

### 7.2 Risks and blockers, with mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation to show |
|---|---|
| **Fettering or dictation of statutory discretion** (Jadeja; Kerala HC; SC Dec 2025): an AI-drafted order signed unread | Officer must act on each flag; no bulk approve for amber cases; drafts contain only record-cited facts; "officer's own findings" box is mandatory for rejections; edit and override logs; periodic override-rate audits |
| **Wrong-authority routing** (HC, Jul 2026) | Role-aware queues: SDO for permanent caste, Tehsildar for income, domicile and temporary caste |
| **DPDP Second Schedule** (from May 2027): purpose limitation, accuracy, notice | Certificate-type-specific field allow-lists; show the applicant which records were used; adverse facts disclosed in the order with a route to contest; 1-year immutable logs; SDC-only processing |
| **No CG state data-sharing policy or SDGC approval yet** | Present an MoU and data-sharing-agreement template; API wrappers per the national framework; sandbox pilot on synthetic data |
| **Bias / "caste profiling"** (surname or geography-based risk scoring could be discriminatory; Fairness sutra) | **No ML risk score on caste identity.** Flags are rule-based, record-inconsistency checks only (e.g., "father's certificate says OBC; applicant claims ST"). Publish flag definitions; monitor flag rates by district and category |
| **Legacy record quality** (pre-1950 records, handwritten jamabandi, name transliteration) | Fuzzy matching with confidence scores; "insufficient data → normal field enquiry (Rule 8)", **never** "insufficient data → reject" |
| **Officer liability, s.12 Caste Act** | Frame the tool as a *due-diligence record* that protects honest officers (s.12 protects due diligence) |
| **Union pushback** (tehsildar unions strike readily) | Position as "staff you don't have" (answers the strike demand for computer operators and technical support), not surveillance. Do not show officer-level leaderboards on slides |
| **Security** (SDC, M-SOC procurement ongoing) | SDC hosting, no external LLM APIs, CERT-In 6-hour reporting, role-based access |
